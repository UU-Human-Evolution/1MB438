## Introduction

This is the last session of the bioinformatics project and will be used to wrap up the project and prepare your presentation.

By now you have assembled a dataset to answer the question from `Lab 1`. You collected *CYTB* sequences and entire mitochondrial genomes for all species in the dataset, aligned them, built a phylogenetic tree and investigated some aspects of it (e.g. the selected model, bootstrap values). Now it is time to reflect on all you have done and answer your research question.

## Build a presentation

On the day of the presentation, we will meet in a lecture hall instead of the computer room. Each group will give a 5-10 minutes slide presentation about their project, where all students in each group should speak for 2-3 minutes each. Not all groups will have chosen the same research question nor they have seen your data before, so remember to be clear (e.g. include common species names instead of only scientific latin names, illustrate your speach with a clear figure, explain the reasoning to your conclusions, etc.).

You should at least:

+ Present your research question to the rest of the class
+ Briefly summarize the data you collected (e.g. list ingroup and outgroup species)
+ Show the phylogenetic trees obtained with *CYTB* gene and the full mitochondrial genome
+ Answer the question of your project (if your results allow)
+ Further discuss your results

This is the perfect time to bring up issues if anything went "wrong" during the analysis (e.g. you're unsure whether a sequence was actually from a certain species because its ID was strange, one sequence was surprisingly longer or shorter than the rest, etc.). You can reflect on the possible consequences this may have on the results, or how you would go about it in a future attempt.

For the discussion of results, here are some points you can pick up in your presentation:

- You built two trees, based on either the *CYTB* gene or the full mitochondrial genome.
   * Do these trees show the same relationship between species? If not, what are the specific differences and what could be an explanation for them?
   * Is the selected substitution model exactly the same, similar, very different? Why could that be the case?
- Think about the species (ingroup and outgroup) that you included in your analyses.
   * Do you think your choice of species was appropriate to answer your research question?
   * If you were to re-do the analysis, would you change anything?
- Briefly examine the bootstrap values.
   * Are some clades supported by all data partitions? What could that mean?
   * Are there any uncertain clades? What could that mean?

## Written report

We encourage all groups to present their results orally in a presentation, so you get a chance to practice your presentation skills as well as see what other groups did, ask questions and discuss their results. If this is not possible, you will need to submit a written report. This decision is individual, so members of a same group can choose different options. In case you choose to submit a report, please inform the teaching assistants and everyone in your group. 

The written report should include the same information as the presentation (see instructions above) but should be structured as a scientific paper, meaning there should be sections for (i) **Introduction**, (ii) **Materials and Methods**, (iii) **Results** and (iv) **Discussion**. You should specify the authors, include citations and reference all figures in the report.

### Hand in the written report

All students who choose to submit a report will have to upload it to Studium in `pdf` format. The deadline for this submission will be after the presentation day.
